STORY 05 • MAHABHARATA (VANA PARVA)

KING SHIBI & THE DOVE

Righteousness & Duty to Protect: Offering one's own flesh to uphold justice, refuge, and the sanctity of life.

Explore the Story ↓

“To grant refuge to a creature seeking protection is the supreme Dharma; even if it costs one's kingdom or body.”

MAHABHARATA LEGEND

Sharṇāgata Rakṣā (Protecting the Refuge-Seeker)

Appearing in the Vana Parva of the Mahabharata, the story of King Shibi is an epic test of Dharma (Righteous Duty) and ethical balance.

When a helpless dove sought shelter in King Shibi's lap while chased by a predatory hawk, the king faced a complex moral dilemma: How to protect the dove without depriving the hawk of its natural sustenance?

THE NARRATIVE

The Trial of Equal Weight

01

The Refuge

A terrified dove flies into King Shibi's court and seeks refuge in his lap, panting for life.

02

The Hawk's Demand

A hawk follows and argues logically: "O King! This dove is my natural prey. If you deny me my food, I will starve, violating my Dharma."

03

The King's Sacred Oath

King Shibi responds: "This creature sought my protection (Sharaṇāgati). Abandoning a seeker of refuge is a grave sin against righteousness."

04

The Ethical Offer

To resolve the conflict, Shibi offers the hawk an equal weight of his own flesh from his body to satisfy its hunger.

05

The Scale Dilemma

A scale is brought. Shibi cuts flesh from his thigh, but no matter how much flesh he cuts, the dove on the other side remains heavier.

06

Step on the Scale

Realizing his flesh is insufficient, King Shibi steps onto the scale himself. Agni (hawk) and Indra (dove) reveal their divine forms and shower blessings for his unmatched virtue.

MORAL DYNAMICS

Resolving Ethical Conflicts

1

Cry for Protection

Accepting responsibility when a weaker being seeks shelter.

2

Competing Duties

Navigating the dilemma between protecting the weak vs satisfying another's legitimate hunger.

3

Personal Accountability

Refusing to shift the burden onto someone else; taking the cost on oneself.

4

Total Dedication

Offering one's entire self when partial measures fall short.

5

Divine Harmony

Achieving eternal moral peace and proving the supreme value of compassion.
